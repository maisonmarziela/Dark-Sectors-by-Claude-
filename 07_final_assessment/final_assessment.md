# Project CRH — Final Scientific Assessment

## Classification

> **Interesting but incomplete.**

The Co-Located Realities Hypothesis can be turned into a mathematically consistent classical effective field theory that reproduces every element of the motivating thought experiment. It cannot at present be called a viable physical theory: three problems that could each invalidate it are unresolved, and the scenario that motivated it (another observer living at "the corresponding location") is disfavoured by the model's own logic. There is no observational evidence for any part of it.

## The strongest original contribution

**A quantitative constraint on transient cross-sector contact**, in four linked statements:

1. *Touch bound.* A coherent contact force between two bodies of ordinary density reaches the threshold of human touch only if the effective cross-sector coupling inside the event is
   G ≳ 1.9 × 10⁴ G_F × (P_touch / 100 Pa) × (ρ_water² / ρ_A ρ_B),
   a contact scale of about 2 GeV. No renormalizable portal within current bounds comes within a factor of 10² of this.
2. *Selection rule.* For portals in which each sector couples separately to mediators, F_AB² ≤ F_AA F_BB. A perceptible cross-sector force without anomalous forces among ordinary bodies therefore requires either enormous self-forces in the other sector or a **product portal**, in which the mediator couples to the product of the two sectors' operators.
3. *Thermal corollary.* A contact coupling of touch strength puts the overlapping bodies in thermal contact, with equilibration in seconds or less, and heat exchange scaling as the square of the traction.
4. *Constructive existence at the classical level.* A product portal with an overlap-triggered gate gives exact classical isolation below a finite-size threshold, a bounded attractive force above it, no residual coupling afterwards, and power-law-suppressed radiation.

Secondary: the cancellation lemma for kinetic mixing between neutral bodies, which appears to conflict with a published estimate; and the observation that an even gate's zero cannot be protected by an internal symmetry.

None of these is deep. All are elementary consequences of standard field theory. Their claim to originality is that we did not find them stated, after a limited search.

## Answers to the brief's specific questions

- **Can two sets of fields share a spacetime and stay decoupled?** Yes — standard. But not *completely* decoupled: at least gravity (or a link field) must be shared for co-location to mean anything.
- **Can the interaction be absent ordinarily and non-zero in a bounded region?** Yes, if it is controlled by a dynamical field. Exactly absent classically (even gate) or suppressed by non-linearity (odd gate).
- **Can momentum be transferred, as a contact-like force, without persistent interaction and without violating conservation or causality?** Yes in the model; verified numerically.
- **Is the literal sensation of touch achievable?** Only with a coupling far beyond the known portals. In the model it would be a pull, with heat.
- **Would gravity necessarily couple the sectors?** With one metric, yes. Bimetric gravity can decouple them at short range, at a cost we did not fully evaluate.
- **Is long-term coexistence stable?** Gravitationally, as stable as dark matter and ordinary matter. With the gate: stable at ordinary densities; not controlled at high densities; possibly unstable microscopically through cross-sector binding.
- **Can the sectors have different temporal behaviour?** Different intrinsic rates and temperatures, yes; different proper time, no (with one metric); opposite thermodynamic arrows, not if they are ever in contact at touch strength.
- **Is there a falsifiable prediction distinguishing this from ordinary hidden-sector models?** Conditional ones (sign, thermal signature, density scaling, size threshold). No event rate is predicted, so the framework is not falsifiable by non-observation.

## What requires independent verification

1. Every proof, by a physicist. None has been reviewed.
2. The reading of Mitra & Foot (2003) and the actual size of the static force with kinetic mixing.
3. Novelty: a systematic literature search for the product-of-densities trigger and the touch bound.
4. References tagged **[mem]** in the literature review (pre-arXiv papers cited from memory).
5. The tactile threshold, from the psychophysics literature.
6. The standard results we used without re-deriving: the bimetric strong-coupling scale, the hidden-mass and fifth-force bounds.
7. The numerical experiments, by an independent implementation (ideally in 3+1 dimensions).

## What would change the classification

- **Upward** (toward "potentially original and defensible"): an explicit ultraviolet completion that passes meson-decay, collider, stellar and cosmological constraints; a demonstration that cross-sector capture is negligible; a natural (e.g. geometrically sequestered) gate; confirmation of novelty.
- **Downward** (toward "inconsistent with established constraints"): a proof that no completion survives; or that capture into cross-sector bound states is unavoidable and catastrophic; or a bound on co-located matter that also closes the bimetric loophole.

## Integrity statement

- No sources, data, proofs or observations were fabricated. Citations were machine-verified where possible and flagged where not.
- The simulations test the model's internal consistency only. They are not evidence about nature and do not demonstrate the existence of other universes.
- No unexplained sensation, anecdote or experience was used as evidence, and none should be read as support for this framework.
- Assumptions are labelled as assumptions; estimates as estimates; and three claims that failed their own tests during the work are recorded as failures in the research journal.
- The adversarial review is a simulation by the same system that wrote the manuscript and is not independent review.
